# Phishing & Social Engineering Risk Assessment

## Project Overview

This project examines phishing and social engineering as significant cybersecurity risks to a fictional enterprise organization. The assessment demonstrates how attackers can exploit human behavior, communication channels, authentication weaknesses, and organizational processes to gain unauthorized access to systems and sensitive information.

The project evaluates phishing attack scenarios, identifies vulnerabilities and indicators of compromise, assesses organizational risk, and develops technical, administrative, and awareness-based controls designed to reduce the likelihood and impact of successful social engineering attacks.

## Objectives

- Identify common phishing and social engineering attack techniques
- Analyze realistic phishing scenarios and indicators of compromise
- Evaluate the likelihood and business impact of social engineering threats
- Identify human, technical, and procedural vulnerabilities
- Recommend preventive and detective security controls
- Develop an employee security awareness strategy
- Establish procedures for responding to successful phishing attacks
- Measure security awareness effectiveness using cybersecurity metrics
- Translate technical security findings into actionable recommendations for leadership

## Threat Landscape

Social engineering attacks target human behavior rather than relying exclusively on technical vulnerabilities. Attackers may use urgency, authority, fear, curiosity, or financial incentives to manipulate individuals into taking actions that compromise organizational security.

Threats evaluated in this project include:

- Email phishing
- Spear phishing
- Business Email Compromise (BEC)
- Credential harvesting
- Malicious links and attachments
- Executive impersonation
- Vendor impersonation
- MFA fatigue attacks
- Pretexting
- Social engineering through phone or messaging platforms

## Assessment Approach

Each scenario is evaluated according to:

- Threat actor objective
- Attack method
- Targeted asset or business process
- Social engineering technique
- Vulnerability exploited
- Indicators of compromise
- Likelihood
- Potential business impact
- Existing security controls
- Recommended mitigation

The assessment considers both technical controls and human factors because effective protection against social engineering requires a layered security strategy.

## Security Controls

Recommended controls include:

- Phishing-resistant multifactor authentication
- Email security filtering
- Domain authentication using SPF, DKIM, and DMARC
- Endpoint detection and response
- URL and attachment analysis
- Least-privilege access
- Out-of-band verification for sensitive transactions
- Employee security awareness training
- Phishing simulations
- Simple employee reporting procedures
- Incident response procedures
